A large screen should make work easier. Yet when every social network lives in a separate window, all that space quickly turns into a pile of tabs, overlapping windows, and repeated context switching.

A conversation is moving on LinkedIn. A community needs an answer on Discord. An Instagram post has to be checked. Between two actions, you need to find the right window, the right account, and the right thread again.

That is why we designed **CommunityGlows Bento** as a real working matrix: a space you can split, resize, and reorganize to keep several networks within view.

It is not a row of shortcuts. It is not a mosaic of frozen screenshots either. The networks remain open and usable inside the cockpit.

## A freeform matrix instead of a fixed dashboard

No two social workflows are organized in exactly the same way.

A community manager may want to watch three networks side by side. A support operator may prefer one large conversation with two smaller spaces nearby. A creator may keep their primary network at the top and inspiration sources underneath.

Bento lets you build that organization directly on screen:

- split an area vertically or horizontally;
- nest several splits inside the same workspace;
- drag a card into another area;
- resize each divider as priorities change;
- group cards when they belong to the same context;
- close or redistribute a panel without rebuilding the whole cockpit.

A regular grid is available, but it is never mandatory. One panel can occupy half the screen while two others share the remaining space, and that hierarchy can change whenever the work changes.

## Multiple networks that are genuinely visible at once

The goal is not to display as much information as possible. It is to bring together information that needs to be understood together.

Each visible panel hosts its own embedded web view, so several networks can stay visible and interactive at the same time. For example, you can:

- keep LinkedIn next to a community inbox;
- monitor a post on one network while drafting a reply on another;
- watch several communities during an important announcement;
- compare information without constantly switching windows;
- give more room to the channel that needs immediate attention.

When a card moves or becomes temporarily hidden, CommunityGlows aims to preserve its context instead of rebuilding the page every time. That continuity matters: reorganizing a cockpit should not create a new cycle of waiting and signing in again.

## Turn screen space into a decision tool

An effective cockpit does not give everything the same visual weight. It helps you see what matters now.

An urgent conversation can take up two thirds of the screen while the networks you are monitoring remain visible in smaller areas. Once the situation settles, the layout can evolve without forcing you to close the other spaces.

Resizing gives priorities a visual language:

- what is large needs action;
- what remains visible needs monitoring;
- what is grouped belongs to the same context;
- what is no longer useful can be closed.

This approach is especially useful on an ultrawide monitor, a large 4K display, or any high-resolution desktop setup. Extra space no longer serves only to make one page bigger. It becomes a decision surface.

## Four layouts to get started quickly

Freedom does not mean starting from a blank canvas every time. CommunityGlows includes four presets that reorganize the cards you already have open:

- **Columns** to monitor several networks side by side;
- **Rows** to stack workspaces vertically;
- **Focus** to enlarge the primary panel while keeping the others close;
- **Grid** to create a balanced overview of multiple networks.

These presets do not replace a custom Bento. They provide an immediate starting point, and every divider remains adjustable afterward.

Most importantly, the existing cards are reused during reorganization. Choosing Grid or Focus does not mean closing your networks and reopening them in a new interface.

## Save a cockpit as a Scene

The right layout often depends on the task. Mornings may be for monitoring, afternoons for replies, and the end of the day for checking published content.

Instead of rebuilding the same organization every time, a Bento can be saved as a **Scene**. You name it once, then card moves, resizes, additions, and removals are saved automatically after a short delay. Local persistence happens before background synchronization, so organizing the cockpit remains fluid when the connection is unreliable.

A named Scene represents a concrete workflow:

- “Morning monitoring”;
- “Community support”;
- “Product launch”;
- “Priority replies”;
- “Client follow-up.”

Loading a Scene restores the networks, their positions, and the proportions of the cockpit. Before switching to another Scene or profile, CommunityGlows flushes the latest pending change. The layout stops being a visual preference and becomes reusable working memory without another save button to remember.

## One profile, its Scenes, and its own context

CommunityGlows separates different contexts such as personal activity, a brand, a client, an association, or a project. Bento respects that boundary.

Each profile has:

- its own saved Scenes;
- its own selected Scene;
- its own current local draft;
- its own isolated network sessions on the device.

Switching profiles should never briefly expose the previous profile's panels. CommunityGlows saves the outgoing draft, clears the cockpit, and restores only the incoming profile's workspace.

Saved Scenes are designed to follow their profile through CommunityGlows sync on connected devices. The current draft and signed-in network sessions remain local. Restoring an organization does not silently transfer social network logins.

That distinction is deliberate. The structure of your work can be synchronized while sensitive sessions stay attached to the device that created them.

## Restore a Scene without restoring just anything

Saving a layout also means being able to read it back safely. CommunityGlows validates a Scene before applying it.

A restored Scene must match networks known to the active profile. Custom links are checked against their exact address. Unknown, insecure, or misleading URLs are rejected, and corrupted data is ignored instead of blocking startup.

Layouts also have explicit size and complexity limits. Those boundaries are not meant to restrict creative arrangements. They prevent an accidental or damaged configuration from making the workspace unstable.

The goal is straightforward: a Scene should save time without becoming a new source of risk or profile confusion.

## Three practical cockpits to try

### The monitoring cockpit

Place three or four networks in a grid, with a slightly larger area for your primary channel. You keep a panoramic view of ongoing conversations without giving every feed the same priority.

### The support cockpit

Use Focus for the active conversation and keep other inboxes in secondary panels. When the priority changes, move or enlarge the new conversation that needs attention.

### The launch cockpit

Display the networks involved in an announcement side by side. Check the publication, watch the first reactions, and respond to communities without losing the overall picture.

These three examples can become separate Scenes. The same screen can then adapt to several working rhythms without forcing one permanent organization.

## Designed for desktop, not merely stretched to fit it

Many interfaces treat a large screen as an enlarged mobile display. Bento takes the opposite approach: it treats available width as a working capability in its own right.

Mouse interaction, drag and drop, resizable dividers, and simultaneous network visibility are not decorative additions. Together, they create an interaction model for people who spend a meaningful part of their day moving between communities.

The goal is not to fill every pixel. It is to create a calm workspace where several networks can coexist without becoming another wall of distractions.

## What comes next?

Bento provides the foundation for a faster social workspace. The next ideas include a global command bar, customizable keyboard shortcuts, and panel duplication.

Those capabilities belong to the next stage of the project and are not part of the Bento described here yet. The direction remains the same: reach the right network, profile, and context faster without turning the interface into a control-room parody.

## Build the cockpit that fits your day

CommunityGlows Bento turns a social dashboard into a flexible workspace. You choose which networks stay within view, how much room each one gets, and which Scenes you want to return to.

On a laptop, it already helps distribute limited space more deliberately. On a large display, it comes into its own: several networks become one working environment organized around your priorities rather than your operating system's windows.

Your screen is large enough to see the whole picture. Your social workspace should be too.
